Abstract:

In the digital age, user-generated content has emerged as a pivotal resource for consumers seeking product information and making informed decisions, especially in multi-attribute decision-making contexts. Existing studies have predominantly focused on textual reviews, while the complementary and expressive value of user-generated photos has often been overlooked. Visual data not only provide contextual cues but also convey rich affective and experiential information that textual content alone cannot fully capture. To address this gap, this study develops a visual-textual fusion framework for multi-attribute product ranking, functioning as a multimodal decision support system that leverages textual reviews and user-generated photos to provide a more comprehensive basis for product evaluation. Specifically, the bootstrapping language-image pre-training model is used to transform user-generated photos into textual descriptions, which are then combined with the original textual reviews to create augmented review texts. Subsequently, the Top2Vec model is applied to the augmented review texts for topic modeling to extract key product attributes. Additionally, a backpropagation neural network is employed to analyze the relative importance of reviews, and a bagging neural network model based on affective distribution computing is implemented to evaluate the weights of product attributes. Finally, incorporating these weights, a regret theory-based ranking model is constructed that accounts for consumer psychological factors in the decision making framework. Empirical analysis using data from a well-known travel platform demonstrates the robustness and interpretability of the proposed framework through comparative experiments, parameter sensitivity analysis, and ablation studies. Benchmark comparisons with classical multi-attribute decision-making methods show that the integration of visual and textual information substantially enhances product ranking performance and provides deeper insights into consumer preferences.
