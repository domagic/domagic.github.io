Abstract:

As artificial intelligence (AI)-driven online education expands rapidly, ensuring high levels of learner satisfaction has become essential for sustaining engagement and improving learning outcomes in increasingly competitive digital learning environments. This study analyzes 66,608 learner reviews from Coursera AI-related courses to identify and predict key determinants of learner satisfaction. Leveraging an integrated natural language processing and machine learning (ML) pipeline, we apply Top2Vec for topic extraction and use Recursive Neural Tensor Network to compute fine-grained affective distributions for each attribute. Eight supervised ML models are tested, with the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) demonstrating the strongest predictive performance. To enhance model transparency, SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) is employed to visualize and interpret how different determinants influence satisfaction. The results reveal five dominant factors, programming difficulty, course content relevance, learning resources & support, learning load, and instructor quality, that collectively shape satisfaction outcomes in AI courses. By quantifying these determinants and their affective dynamics, this study deepens theoretical understanding of satisfaction formation and provides actionable insights for improving course design, platform support, and instructional strategies in AI-enabled online learning.

Keywords: Distance education and online learning; Learner satisfaction; Unstructured learner reviews; Explainable ML; Affective distribution computing
