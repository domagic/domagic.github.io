## 1. Research phenomenon: If both formats sell through video, where does the difference come from?

A presenter describes the fabric and size of a sweater on camera, and viewers at home decide whether to order. This scene fits both TV Home Shopping (TVHS) and Livestream Shopping (LSS). Both share basic elements such as video demonstration, presenter explanation and remote purchasing. Judged by these elements alone, livestream shopping looks like an internet version of TV shopping.

The difference emerges when viewers want to learn more about a product. TV home shopping audiences usually follow a scheduled programme; some programmes allow phone or text feedback, and the presenter chooses which questions to answer. In a livestream, viewers can ask about sizing during the demonstration, request another angle, and see the messages and replies of others in the same session. Product information therefore comes both from the streamer's prepared presentation and from live questions and feedback. Whether viewers can participate in generating information, and whether other viewers can influence that process, is the starting point for judging whether the two formats can be explained by a single mechanism.

## 2. Research question: Is livestream shopping a distinct object of study?

The paper breaks the question of whether livestream shopping is conceptually new into three connected questions. First, what do the two channels share in definition and function, who sends information, and who can respond? Second, which theories do existing studies adopt, and which variables do they place as antecedents, mediators, moderators and consequences? Third, can these comparisons show that livestream shopping needs an independent explanatory framework?

The authors split the systematic literature review into two parallel sub-studies. Study one characterizes 42 livestream shopping papers, and study two characterizes 29 TV home shopping papers. Both sets are organized through the same comparison dimensions and then interpreted with an integrated framework to explain similarities and differences. The unit of analysis is published research and its variables, not a single group of viewers completing shopping tasks in both formats.

## 3. Scientific questions and design rationale

**Table 2. Three levels of conceptual comparison**

| Scientific question | Object of comparison | How it is used to judge conceptual difference |
|---|---|---|
| How does communication structure change? | Sender, medium, receiver, and the flow of information | Examine whether viewers can respond immediately and access the feedback of others in the same session |
| How are consumer responses formed? | Theoretical orientation, antecedents, mediators and moderators | Compare whether studies start more from the streamer-viewer relationship or from channel functionality |
| What outcomes do studies seek to explain? | Purchase, satisfaction, loyalty, engagement, sharing, and more | Judge whether participation and relational outcomes beyond the transaction become objects of study |

These three levels move from communication conditions toward behavioural explanation. The medium changes who can provide and receive feedback; feedback structure shapes how interpersonal relationships and channel evaluations form, which in turn changes which variables researchers use to explain transactions and engagement. Even when both sets of papers study trust, one must distinguish whether consumers trust the streamer, the product or the shopping channel in order to judge whether they are studying the same process.

## 4. Methods and integrated framework at a glance

### 4.1 How were the two bodies of literature screened through the same process?

![Figure 2. Systematic review procedure](assets/2024-JBR-Is-livestream-shopping-conceptually-new/figure-02.png)

**Figure 2. Overview of our systematic review procedure.**

Figure 2 follows the Scientific Procedures and Rationales for Systematic Literature Reviews (SPAR-4-SLR), arranged top to bottom in three stages.

1. **Assembling.** The authors searched Scopus and Web of Science for titles, abstracts and keywords related to livestream shopping or TV shopping. After deduplication, 231 papers remained. Google Scholar added 165 more, for a total of 396. The time span runs from 1990 to July 2022 and includes forthcoming papers. The scope is limited to English-language, peer-reviewed articles in journals indexed in CABS or ABDC, focusing on consumer behaviour in shopping contexts.
2. **Arranging.** The authors extracted theories, antecedents, mediators, moderators and consequences in a consistent way, recording publication year, method, geography and platform. The team excluded 324 papers on off-topic subjects and one review to avoid double counting, leaving 71 studies.
3. **Assessing.** Content analysis was used to categorize the theories and variables in both groups and to synthesize where they converge and where they differ in emphasis. The paper also tallies the methods and market settings, helping readers understand the research contexts behind the conclusions.

### 4.2 How should the integrated framework of sender, medium and receiver be read?

![Figure 3. Integrated framework of the livestream shopping and TV home shopping literatures](assets/2024-JBR-Is-livestream-shopping-conceptually-new/figure-03.png)

**Figure 3. An integrative framework: Comparison of LSS and TVHS literatures.**

Figure 3 moves left to right through the Sender, Media, Receiver and research mechanisms. The arrows in the upper half can be read in both directions. The streamer passes product information to viewers, viewers can ask questions immediately, and other viewers can see the relevant replies. The streamer may be an influencer, a celebrity, a company executive, or an ordinary platform user. Viewers can therefore help shape the presentation, for example by asking the streamer to explain the details they care about most. The right side summarizes this structure as a Marketing Tool for Engagement, centred on engagement and relationship formation.

The arrows in the lower half run mainly from the presenter to the viewers. TV home shopping programmes use professional presenters or celebrities to display products; the programme provides useful information and facilitates purchase. Some viewers can give feedback by phone, but the production team decides which feedback is broadcast, and other viewers have fewer opportunities to access real-time replies. The right side therefore characterizes TV home shopping as a Direct Selling Channel, centred on the product and the transaction.

The arrows indicate information flows. They help explain why the two channels develop different research emphases; evidence for the underlying mechanisms still comes from the primary studies summarized below.

## 5. Comparison results

### 5.1 Common ground: Video introduction, information value and purchase outcomes

Both bodies of literature emphasize product demonstration and remote purchase, and both study utilitarian value, convenience and hedonic value. At the theoretical level, the Elaboration Likelihood Model and Media Richness Theory appear in both groups. Purchase intention, purchase behaviour, impulse buying, satisfaction and loyalty also overlap. These commonalities explain why TV home shopping can serve as a meaningful reference for livestream shopping research.

### 5.2 Antecedents: Livestream research attends more to the streamer and co-viewers

![Figure 4. Distribution of antecedents in the livestream shopping and TV home shopping literatures](assets/2024-JBR-Is-livestream-shopping-conceptually-new/figure-04.png)

**Figure 4. Antecedents in the LSS versus TVHS literatures.**

The colour of each bar denotes a category of antecedent, the number outside the parentheses is the coded frequency for that category, and the number inside is its share among all antecedents in that group. In the livestream shopping literature, streamer-related antecedents account for 54% (68/126), the largest category, such as streamer attractiveness, expertise or interaction quality. In the TV home shopping literature, channel-related antecedents account for 38% (19/50), the largest category, such as information richness, payment convenience and utilitarian value. Co-viewer antecedents appear nine times in the livestream literature and are not coded in the TV shopping studies included.

### 5.3 Mediators: The same label of trust, different objects of trust

![Figure 5. Distribution of mediators in the livestream shopping and TV home shopping literatures](assets/2024-JBR-Is-livestream-shopping-conceptually-new/figure-05.png)

**Figure 5. Mediators in the LSS versus TVHS literatures.**

In the livestream shopping literature, streamer-related mediators account for 39% (29/75), such as streamer trust, parasocial interaction, swift guanxi and tie strength. In the TV home shopping literature, channel-related mediators account for 28% (7/25), such as reliance on TV shopping, convenience and perceived risk. The remaining three categories each account for 24%, so the channel category is the largest in that group, only four percentage points above the others.

If a streamer's immediate reply relates to purchase through streamer trust, trust describes how consumers evaluate a person. If sufficient product information and convenient payment relate to shopping attitude through channel trust, trust describes how consumers evaluate a transactional environment. Placing both under the single label of trust can obscure this distinction.

### 5.4 Theories, moderators and outcomes: From channel functionality to social relationships

**Table 3. Main comparison results of the two bodies of literature**

| Dimension | Livestream shopping literature | TV home shopping literature |
|---|---|---|
| Theoretical orientation | Mixed Orientation is the largest coded category. Common frameworks are Stimulus-Organism-Response and affordance theory, and relationship formation is also often explained through attachment, social exchange and parasocial interaction | Studies that use theory more often take a Media Orientation, such as media dependency and media richness. Of the 29 studies, 19 do not explicitly use a theory |
| Mechanism emphasis | Streamer interaction style, streamer trust, co-viewer feedback and relationship building | Transmission of product information, convenience, payment, channel dependency and perceived risk |
| Moderator examples | Platform type, programme popularity, viewing duration or membership tenure | Gender, culture, loneliness and other consumer characteristics |
| Outcome emphasis | Purchase, satisfaction and loyalty, along with engagement, sharing, stickiness and willingness to send virtual gifts | Purchase, satisfaction and loyalty, along with programme viewing and purchase frequency |

The authors therefore conclude that livestream shopping is conceptually new. It continues video demonstration and direct selling while embedding synchronous, mutually visible communication into the shopping process, bringing the relationship between the streamer and co-viewers into the explanatory frame. Among the studies reviewed, livestream shopping mechanisms are explained mainly through social and relational processes, whereas TV home shopping is more often explained through the functionality of the channel itself.

## 6. Advanced analysis

### 6.1 How should literature frequencies and theoretical proportions be read?

The 42 and 29 in the review are counts of papers. The 126 and 50 in Figure 4 are the total counts of antecedent codes extracted from the corresponding literatures, and the 75 and 25 in Figure 5 are the mediator code totals. A single paper can use streamer attractiveness, synchronicity and perceived value at once, and may test several mediators. Therefore, "54% of livestream antecedents concern the streamer" describes the **composition of the codes**. To read it as a proportion of papers, one would have to recount using the number of papers containing each category of variable.

A similar difference of units appears in the theoretical results. The 42 livestream shopping studies used 29 different theories, with the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework used seven times. Mixed Orientation accounts for 44.2% of the coded theory applications and Social Orientation for 21.2%. A mixed framework can include technological, streamer and consumer variables at once, so mixed orientation being the largest theoretical category does not conflict with the overall judgment that social and relational mechanisms are emphasized. Review frequencies describe where research directs its attention; they cannot directly compare the effect sizes produced by different classes of variables.

### 6.2 Using antecedents, mediators, moderators and consequences to unpack an explanatory chain

Take interaction quality in livestreaming as an example. Whether a streamer responds promptly and gives useful answers to viewers' questions is an antecedent. Interaction can foster swift guanxi, an understanding and rapport formed in a short time, which in turn relates to purchase intention. In such a model, swift guanxi is the mediator, explaining how interaction translates into purchase intention.

Moderators answer conditional questions. For example, the studies examined whether room popularity and membership tenure change the link between interaction and tie strength. Purchase intention, engagement or sharing are outcomes. When comparing with TV shopping, the same analytical grammar can be used. The richness of information or convenience of payment in a television programme is an antecedent, and channel trust or attitude can explain how these connect to subsequent adoption intention. Read this way, both sets of studies use the same analytical roles while placing different objects and processes into those roles.

### 6.3 How should the relationship between interaction technology and consumer empowerment be understood?

Technological affordance is the range of actions a medium makes possible, such as viewers asking questions, seeing others' feedback or requesting additional demonstration. Consumer Empowerment concerns whether consumers actually gain control over information and the shopping experience. The two can be linked as a chain to be tested. Interactive features provide opportunities for action; whether the streamer responds and whether other feedback is visible affect whether consumers obtain the information they need, after which they may develop a stronger sense of control and change their engagement or purchase behaviour.

This decomposition explains why the authors list consumer empowerment as a future research topic. It can be measured as a standalone variable to determine when the interactive capabilities of a livestream truly become consumer choice.

### 6.4 What questions for further testing does the review propose?

**Table 4. Livestream shopping research directions proposed by the authors**

| Direction | What can be tested specifically |
|---|---|
| Segment livestream shopping mechanisms | Compare how platform, source of streamer authority, content format and product category change the formation of engagement and purchase |
| Validate consumer empowerment | Test whether felt autonomy and control connect interactive features to subsequent engagement and purchase |
| Use objective transaction data and new methods | Combine actual sales and viewing behaviour, or use image and video analysis to identify visual features related to engagement |
| Conduct cross-cultural research | Compare viewer responses to the streamer, interaction and transaction across languages and market settings |
| Study regulation and consumer protection | Examine how commercial disclosure, platform governance and consumer protection measures affect trust and shopping behaviour |

These directions start from the mechanisms identified by the review. They translate the conceptual features of livestream shopping into observable questions, such as who asks questions, whether answers are given, whether consumers can obtain additional information, and how these changes relate to actual transactions.
