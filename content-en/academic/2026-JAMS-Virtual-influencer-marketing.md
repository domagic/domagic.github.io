## 1. Research phenomenon: Virtual endorsers attract attention, so why might they not be more persuasive?

Brands can invite human bloggers to try a product, or they can have a virtual influencer post the same promotional content. A virtual character may seem fresh because its appearance or identity defies the audience's expectations. Yet when the character goes on to claim that a particular lipstick is moisturising or that a certain food tastes good, viewers also judge whether it can offer a credible experience of use. The same virtual design may therefore bring both an advantage in novelty and a disadvantage in credibility at the same time.

Virtual influencers also differ in their degree of anthropomorphism. Some characters resemble real people in appearance and behaviour, while others are clearly designed in a cartoonish style. Even when endorsing the same product, the two may lead audiences to rely on different evaluative criteria. A highly anthropomorphic character more readily calls to mind the standards we apply to human endorsers, whereas a stylised character may attract audiences through its distinctive image. The question brands actually face is therefore: **what are the advantages and costs of virtual versus human endorsement, and under what combinations do these differences change?**

## 2. Research questions: Average differences, moderating contexts and persuasion pathways

1. **What is the overall effect?** In experiments that include a human influencer as a comparison, how do virtual influencers perform on behavioural intention, engagement, brand and ad attitudes, and on credibility, attractiveness and novelty respectively?
2. **What conditions change the relative effect?** Which autonomy settings, brand positions, product types and message appeals fit highly anthropomorphic and weakly anthropomorphic characters respectively?
3. **What does persuasion rely on?** How do credibility and novelty connect to consumer behavioural outcomes through attitudes or directly? How do these pathways differ between high and low anthropomorphism and between earlier and later published studies?

These three questions correspond to the overall effect, moderation and mediation pathways respectively. For example, higher novelty is an overall comparison result. A cartoonish character performing relatively well when using an emotional appeal is the result of a conditional combination. How novelty further connects to attitude and behaviour requires a separate answer from a path model.

## 3. Scientific question and theoretical framework

## 4. A quick look at the research methods

### 4.1 From experimental literature to a common effect size

The authors searched multiple literature databases and unpublished studies, and traced the bibliographies of relevant work. By the end of 2025 they had found 1,289 candidate papers, of which 110 papers, 210 independent experiments and 643 effect sizes were included after screening, with a total consumer sample of 91,058. The included studies provide a computable comparison between virtual and human endorsement. A single experiment may measure ad attitude, purchase intention and credibility at the same time, so one study can contribute several effect sizes.

The authors standardised the effects as correlation coefficients and corrected for measurement error in the outcome variables. When reading the results, a positive value means the virtual group scored higher than the human group on the corresponding measure, and a negative value means it scored lower. Each category of outcome was summarised separately, avoiding a single average that would mask the combination of "higher novelty but lower behavioural intention".

### 4.2 What does each of the three analysis modules address?

**Table 3. Analysis modules and their purposes**

| Module | Analytical input | Question addressed |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchical pooling of effect sizes | Experimental contrast effects between virtual and human endorsement | What is the average difference for each category of response, and is there heterogeneity between studies? |
| Three-level meta-regression | Effect sizes, marketing conditions, outcome types and study attributes | With which combinations of anthropomorphism and other conditions does the relative effect differ? |
| Meta-analytic structural equation modelling (MASEM) | Pooled correlation matrices for each group across studies | How are credibility, novelty, attitudes and behaviour related, and which pathways differ between groups? |

Moderators were coded independently by two coders, with an overall agreement rate of 91%. High autonomy was coded according to how the character was presented in the experimental materials, referring to a character described as generating content or interacting autonomously with the help of AI. Low autonomy refers to a character that relies on human operation and external management. For sensory cues, proximal refers to experiences such as taste and touch that normally require closeness or contact, while distal refers to experiences such as sight and hearing that can be obtained at a distance. Here proximal and distal distinguish types of sensory experience.

## 5. Research findings

### 5.1 Pooled effects: Higher novelty, lower credibility and several persuasion outcomes

**Table 4. Average effects of virtual relative to human influencers**

| Consumer response | Number of effect sizes | Corrected weighted correlation | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Behavioural intention | 199 | −0.130 | Higher in the human group |
| Engagement | 81 | −0.052 | Difference not reported as significant in the main text |
| Brand attitude | 82 | −0.120 | Higher in the human group |
| Ad attitude | 51 | −0.192 | Higher in the human group |
| Credibility | 175 | −0.234 | Higher in the human group |
| Attractiveness | 30 | −0.160 | Difference not significant according to the main text and the reported confidence interval |
| Novelty | 28 | 0.217 | Higher in the virtual group |

The 95% confidence interval for credibility is [−0.321, −0.146], and for novelty [0.060, 0.374]. The two point in opposite directions and can hold at the same time. Behavioural intention includes intentions such as purchase or recommendation measured in experiments and cannot be converted directly into a real sales difference. The paper reports significant heterogeneity tests for all outcomes, so examining marketing conditions further is practically meaningful.

### 5.2 Contextual fit: The same communication condition can produce different results for the two character types

![Figure 3. Interaction plots of anthropomorphism and five marketing conditions](assets/2026-JAMS-Virtual-influencer-marketing/figure-03.png)

**Figure 3. Interaction effect plots.**

In Figure 3, the blue line represents high anthropomorphism and the grey line low anthropomorphism. The horizontal axis shows the two values of each condition, and the vertical axis the average effect of the virtual relative to the human group, with the dashed zero line indicating equivalence. Different slopes mean that changes in a condition relate differently to the effectiveness of the two character types. When reading the plots, movement towards zero also counts as an improvement in relative performance, even if the value remains below zero.

**Table 5. Interaction results for anthropomorphism and marketing conditions**

| Condition contrast | Interaction estimate | $p$ value | Main pattern shown in the figure |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| High vs. low autonomy | 0.320 | 0.036 | High anthropomorphism is relatively more advantageous under high autonomy, low anthropomorphism under low autonomy |
| Futuristic brand vs. traditional brand | 0.344 | 0.001 | High anthropomorphism fits futuristic brands better, low anthropomorphism performs relatively better with traditional brands |
| New vs. familiar product | 0.216 | 0.025 | High anthropomorphism combined with a new product performs relatively well, low anthropomorphism has a smaller disadvantage with familiar products |
| Emotional appeal vs. rational appeal | −0.426 | 0.039 | Low anthropomorphism is relatively more advantageous under emotional appeals, while high anthropomorphism differs little between the two appeals |
| Proximal vs. distal sensory cues | 0.363 | 0.012 | High anthropomorphism matches proximal cues better, low anthropomorphism matches distal cues better |

Taking brand positioning as an example, high anthropomorphism yields −0.015 and −0.201 under futuristic and traditional brands respectively. The corresponding values for low anthropomorphism are −0.588 and −0.006. The two lines change in different directions. The multivariate meta-regression further controls for the other coded variables, and Table 5 lists the interaction coefficients from that model. The paper found no significant interactions for brand type, brand communication style, whether the product is used for self-improvement, product involvement or narrative perspective. In the analyses split by outcome category, the interactions of autonomy, brand positioning and sensory cues are significant across all three outcome groups, whereas the interactions of appeal and product novelty vary by outcome category.

### 5.3 Persuasion mechanisms: The relative weight of the two pathways changes with anthropomorphism and research period

**Table 6. Representative standardised paths for high and low anthropomorphism and for earlier and later studies**

| Path | High anthropomorphism | Low anthropomorphism | Recent (from 2023) | Earlier (up to 2022) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Virtual identity → credibility | −0.088 | −0.215 | −0.115 | −0.250 |
| Virtual identity → novelty | 0.205 | 0.580 | 0.197 | 0.581 |
| Credibility → attitude | 0.407 | 0.239 | 0.408 | 0.261 |
| Credibility → behavioural outcome | 0.227 | 0.214 | 0.238 | 0.209 |
| Novelty → attitude | 0.228 | 0.407 | 0.228 | 0.396 |
| Novelty → behavioural outcome | 0.106 | 0.307 | 0.109 | 0.258 |
| Attitude → behavioural outcome | 0.409 | 0.307 | 0.408 | 0.304 |

**By anthropomorphism**, the high anthropomorphism group has a smaller credibility disadvantage and a stronger link between credibility and attitude. The low anthropomorphism group has a larger novelty advantage, and stronger paths from novelty to both attitude and behavioural outcomes. **By research period**, recent studies show a smaller novelty advantage than earlier studies, a smaller credibility disadvantage, and a stronger relationship between credibility and attitude. The direct path from credibility to behavioural outcome shows no significant difference in either group comparison.

The authors summarise the results as a relative shift in the basis of persuasion from novelty to credibility. Studies were grouped by publication year, comparing the experimental relationships pooled across the two periods. The temporal change described here therefore refers to a cross-study pattern. Habituation, mentioned in H2, is the theoretical rationale for this pattern.

## 6. Advanced analysis

### 6.1 How is an average effect estimated from multiple experiments?

Let $r_{ij}$ denote the $i$th effect size reported by study $j$. The average effect model can be written as

$$
r_{ij}=\gamma_{00}+u_{0j}+e_{ij}.
\tag{1}
$$

$\gamma_{00}$ is the overall average effect for the same outcome category, $u_{0j}$ is the part of study $j$'s average effect that deviates from the overall mean, and $e_{ij}$ is the residual variation of an individual effect size within a study. Because several effect sizes from the same experiment share a sample and a design, the model lets them share a single study-level component. The paper uses reliability-corrected weighted correlations and further uses the $Q$ statistic to check whether the observed variation exceeds sampling fluctuation.

The authors also report the fail-safe N, Egger's test and a funnel plot. Outcomes other than novelty show no significant asymmetry in Egger's test. They also compare conclusions for credibility and brand attitude with and without outlying effect sizes, and ultimately retain these observations.

### 6.2 How is the anthropomorphism interaction term in the meta-regression read?

The meta-regression nests effect sizes within studies and studies within papers. It pools different outcome types, distinguishing them through an outcome-category variable, and excludes novelty, whose average performance pattern differs, from this step. Let $A=1$ denote high anthropomorphism, $A=0$ low anthropomorphism, and $X$ one of the binary conditions. Omitting the remaining controls and random terms, this can be written as

$$
\widehat r(A,X)=\beta_0+\beta_A A+\beta_X X+\beta_{AX}AX.
\tag{2}
$$

In the low anthropomorphism group, changing $X$ from 0 to 1 changes the predicted effect by $\beta_X$. In the high anthropomorphism group it changes by $\beta_X+\beta_{AX}$. Thus $\beta_{AX}$ answers how much the conditional difference differs between the two groups.

Taking brand positioning as an example, $X=1$ denotes futuristic. The table's $\beta_X=-0.330$ is the estimated difference for the low anthropomorphism reference group under this contrast. Adding the interaction term $0.344$ gives an estimated difference of $0.014$ for the high anthropomorphism group. This calculation uses the coefficients of the multivariate meta-regression. Figure 3 uses the average effects under each condition to show how the lines change. The common message of the two is that switching from a traditional to a futuristic brand reduces the relative effect more sharply for low anthropomorphism.

### 6.3 How does MASEM link credibility, novelty, attitudes and behaviour?

The authors obtained or converted correlations between variables from each experiment, formed pooled correlation matrices separately by anthropomorphism and period, and then estimated multi-group structural equation models. Attitudes combine brand and ad attitudes, and behavioural outcomes combine behavioural intention and engagement. Let $V$ denote the virtual identity contrast, $C$ credibility, $N$ novelty, $T$ attitude and $B$ behavioural outcome. The paper's seven connections are abbreviated with path coefficients as follows.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C&=a_C V+\varepsilon_C,\qquad N=a_N V+\varepsilon_N,\\
T&=b_C C+b_N N+\varepsilon_T,\\
B&=d_C C+d_N N+gT+\varepsilon_B.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

Equation (3) separates two kinds of influence. $a_C$ and $a_N$ indicate the direction and magnitude of the change in evaluation brought about by virtual identity. $b_C$ and $b_N$ indicate how the two kinds of evaluation connect to attitude. $d_C$, $d_N$ and $g$ indicate the paths from evaluation and attitude to behavioural outcomes. Tracing along linear paths, the same mechanism can connect to behaviour directly or first via attitude.

$$
IE_C=a_Cd_C+a_Cb_Cg,\qquad IE_N=a_Nd_N+a_Nb_Ng.
\tag{4}
$$

For example, in the high anthropomorphism group, the coefficient product for $V\to C\to T$ is approximately $(-0.088)\times0.407=-0.036$. Although credibility and attitude are positively related, the first leg from virtual identity to credibility is negative, so this indirect contribution is also negative. In the low anthropomorphism group, $V\to N\to T$ is approximately $0.580\times0.407=0.236$, positive in direction. Equation (4) illustrates how to read signs and contributions along the paths; between-group statistical tests should be interpreted according to the tests reported in the paper.

### 6.4 What do between-group tests and period grouping tell us?

The Wald test compares whether the coefficients of the same path differ statistically between two groups. The difference in the "virtual identity → credibility" path between high and low anthropomorphism has $p=0.025$, and that of the "virtual identity → novelty" path has $p=0.001$. In the recent versus earlier comparison, the corresponding $p$ values are 0.015 and 0.001. These tests target differences in specific paths between groups. Only by considering the direction and magnitude of the two paths and their subsequent links to attitude and behaviour can a full account of the dual-path mechanism be formed.
